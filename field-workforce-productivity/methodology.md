# Methodology — Field Workforce Productivity & Revenue Optimization

## 1. Establish a common KPI framework

Define a metric dictionary with numerator, denominator, grain, refresh cadence, owner, and intended action. Examples include inspections per standard workday, work-hour utilization, schedule gaps, cost per inspection, and PTO-related capacity loss.

## 2. Find recoverable capacity

Compare scheduled capacity with completed field activity. Segment gaps by reason, time window, geography, and operational constraint. Identify where rescheduling or coverage redistribution can recover work without simply shifting the bottleneck elsewhere.

## 3. Prioritize customer engagement

Segment accounts by lifecycle and activity signals (for example, new, declining, inactive, or reactivated). Align outreach and incentive programs with field capacity and clear eligibility and measurement rules.

## 4. Translate opportunity into inspection targets

Estimate dealer-level potential, compare it with current activity, and convert the gap into monthly inspection opportunities. Review targets with field leaders to account for capacity and market realities.

## 5. Quantify commercial impact carefully

Use a transparent calculation:

`Expected fee opportunity = incremental inspections × sell-through rate × average fee per sold unit`

Label assumptions explicitly. Distinguish:
- Capacity recovered
- Incremental inspections completed
- Units sold
- Incremental fees realized
- Forecasted opportunity

Use a counterfactual or comparison group where possible to estimate incremental impact.

## 6. Operate and improve

Use recurring reviews to assign owners, track interventions, monitor leading indicators, and compare actual outcomes with assumptions. Revisit incentives and targets when market conditions or field capacity change.

## Validation note

The monthly estimates in the README are calculations from user-supplied assumptions. They are not independently audited, and initiatives may overlap.
